Target Leakage

Fatal Flaw: In production at 07:00 AM, ops does not know how many casual and registered riders will arrive next hour!

2. The 3 Biggest Demand Drivers ("The WHY") & Required Ops Actions

Driver 1: Time-of-Day & Commuter Pulse (54% Impact)

  • The Why: Weekday demand is bi-modal, driven almost entirely by registered commuters. 08:00 AM averages 477 rides/hr and 17:00-18:00 PM averages 525 rides/hr, while overnight drops below 15 rides/hr.
  • Required Ops Action:
    1. 05:30 - 07:30 AM Morning Inflow Buffer: Mobilize 6 primary rebalancing trucks to inject at least 180 bikes into residential transit docks before commuters leave home.
    2. 15:30 - 17:00 PM Commercial Clear-Out: Station roving trucks at downtown corporate hubs to clear 40% dock capacity before evening returns cause lockouts.

Driver 2: Thermal Comfort & Temperature Gradient (26% Impact)

  • The Why: Demand scales strongly between 10°C and 27°C (+18.4 rides per +1°C rise). Below 8°C, casual demand collapses by 74%, and registered commuter volume drops by 32%.
  • Required Ops Action:
    1. Dynamic Shift Sizing: Scale active driver shift numbers based on the 24-hour weather forecast. For days above 20°C, schedule +2 standby drivers for the afternoon leisure rush.
    2. Cold Weather Fuel Conservation: On freezing days (<5°C), cancel 50% of off-peak roving routes and focus exclusively on core 8 AM / 5 PM transit corridors.

Driver 3: Weather Severity & Precipitation Penalty (14% Impact)

  • The Why: Rain triggers immediate modal substitution into transit and taxis. Light rain causes a 15-25% demand drop; active thunderstorms slash demand by 65-85%.
  • Required Ops Action:
    1. Weather Stand-Down Protocol: When rain probability exceeds 75% for an upcoming operational window, cancel scheduled redistribution sweeps to eliminate wasted driver hours and fuel.
    2. Post-Storm Rebalancing: Immediately deploy maintenance crews post-storm to dry dock electronics and clear fallen debris before fair-weather demand snaps back.

3. Operations Truth: Unmasking the Two Analytical Traps

  1. The 'Perfect Model' Trap (casual & registered): Including casual and registered columns creates an artificial R² = 1.000 because casual + registered == cnt. In production, dispatchers cannot know rider category breakdowns beforehand. Our Dual-Head model forecasts each stream using strictly antecedent calendar and weather data.
  2. The 'Quietly Cheating' Random Split: Random shuffling yields an artificial MAE ≈ 24 by interpolating between neighboring hours. Our strict chronological holdout split proves true real-world performance (MAE ≈ 42), ensuring ops is never blindsided by field conditions.
